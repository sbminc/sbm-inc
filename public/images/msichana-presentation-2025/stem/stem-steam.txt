STEM & STEAM / Science, Technology, Engineering, Math & Intersections with Art or Innovation


Talitha Washington


Mathematician and academic specializing in applied mathematics and STEM education policy. (Wikipedia)


In 2023 she became president of the Association for Women in Mathematics.


Fun fact / quote idea: She works at the intersection of pure math and educational systems design.


Novella Bridges


Chemical engineer, researcher in radiochemistry and advocate for underrepresented groups in STEM. (Wikipedia)


She worked in national labs and on nuclear research.


Fun fact / quote idea: She emphasizes that high-impact research and representation go hand in hand.


Tanya Moore


Biostatistician, mathematics educator, and advocate for women of color in math and science. (Wikipedia)


She co-founded the Infinite Possibilities Conference to support minority representation in math.


Fun fact / quote idea: She has been recognized as a “STEM Woman of the Year” in her region.


K. Renee Horton


Physicist and materials scientist, working with NASA as an Airworthiness Deputy. (Wikipedia)


She was the first Black person to receive a PhD in material science + engineering with emphasis in physics at the University of Alabama.


Fun fact / quote idea: She champions STEM access for people with disabilities and underrepresented backgrounds.


Hadiyah‑Nicole Green


Medical physicist and researcher developing cancer therapies using laser‑activated nanoparticles. (Wikipedia)


She is one of very few Black women in the U.S. with a PhD in physics.


Fun fact / quote idea: Her work combines physics, medicine, and innovation to combat cancer.


Donna Auguste


Engineer, entrepreneur, and inventor. She contributed to early Apple technologies and later founded her own ventures. (MakerGirl)


Her background combines hardware, software, and social impact.


Fun fact / quote idea: She holds patents and has invested in STEM access for girls.


Dr. Patricia Bath


Ophthalmologist, inventor, and pioneer. She invented the laserphaco probe for cataract removal, earning one of the first medical patents by a Black woman. (MakerGirl)


She also made history as the first Black woman faculty member in ophthalmology at UCLA.


Fun fact / quote idea: Her invention improved outcomes for millions with vision loss.


Dr. Nia Imara


Astrophysicist, artist, and educator who integrates science and art to reach underrepresented students. (The Guardian)


She founded Onaketa, a nonprofit providing STEM tutoring for youth of color.


Fun fact / quote idea: She uses cosmic imagery and art to inspire new perspectives on science.
