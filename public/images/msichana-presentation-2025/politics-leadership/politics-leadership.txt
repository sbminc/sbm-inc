1. Shirley Chisholm
Who she was: The first Black woman elected to the United States Congress (1968) and the first Black woman to run for U.S. President with a major party (1972).


Why she matters: Chisholm was a fearless advocate for civil rights, education, and women’s rights.


Quote: "If they don't give you a seat at the table, bring a folding chair."



2. Stacey Abrams
Who she is: Political leader, voting rights activist, and founder of Fair Fight Action, which works to combat voter suppression.


Why she matters: Her organizing is widely credited with increasing voter turnout in Georgia and helping flip key elections.


Fun fact: She’s also a best-selling author and a former candidate for Governor of Georgia.



3. Kamala Harris
Who she is: The first Black and South Asian woman to serve as Vice President of the United States.


Why she matters: She previously served as U.S. Senator and California’s Attorney General, with a strong focus on criminal justice reform, economic equity, and public health.


Quote: "My mother would look at me and she’d say, ‘Kamala, you may be the first to do many things, but make sure you’re not the last.’"



4. Angela Davis
Who she is: Scholar, activist, and former political prisoner known for her work in civil rights, prison abolition, and racial justice.


Why she matters: A lifelong political activist, Davis has inspired generations through her teaching and organizing.


Fun fact: She was on the FBI’s Most Wanted list in the 1970s for her activism—and was later acquitted and became a symbol of resistance.



5. Ayanna Pressley
Who she is: U.S. Representative from Massachusetts and the first Black woman elected to Congress from her state.


Why she matters: A member of "The Squad," she’s known for championing criminal justice reform, reproductive rights, and student debt relief.


Quote: "I'm not here just to occupy space—I'm here to create it."
